persuasion: encourages a target behavior by appealing to PFs such as liking, reciprocation, social proof, consistency, scarcity, and authority
pretexting: creates a plausible scenario or pretext to increase the receiver's engagement with the sender
impersonation: assumes a false identity to increase the receiver's compliance
visual deception: reuses benign-looking visual elements such as logos, URLs, or layouts to make the message appear more legitimate
incentive and motivator: encourages compliance by offering external rewards or internal gratification
urgency: pressures the receiver to act quickly, reducing careful reasoning and increasing compliance
attention grabbing: uses salient visual, textual, or auditory cues to draw the receiver's attention to the message
personalization: uses receiver-specific information to tailor the message and increase perceived relevance and credibility
contextualization: frames the sender as part of the receiver's group or context to establish commonality
quid pro quo: offers something in exchange for the receiver taking an action or risk
foot-in-the-door: gains compliance with a small request first, then escalates to a larger request
trusted relationship: leverages an existing trusted relationship with an entity, service, or person
affection trust: establishes an affectionate or romantic relationship to increase the receiver's willingness to comply
decoy effect: makes the receiver believe they are receiving an unusually attractive deal
priming: gradually influences a later decision through repeated or subtle prior exposure
loss aversion: leverages the receiver's attachment to something they already received or value, making them more willing to act to avoid losing it
