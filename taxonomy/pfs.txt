# Social Psychology
liking: tendency to react positively to those one likes or perceives as similar
reciprocation: tendency to feel obligated to return a favor
social proof: tendency to imitate others' behaviors
consistency: tendency to remain committed to a prior decision, promise, behavior, or task
authority: tendency to obey experts, superiors, or authoritative figures
scarcity: tendency to place higher value on items that are limited in availability
disobedience: willful refusal to follow authority or rules
respect: esteem for another based on perceived worth, which may prevent questioning their requests
perceptual contrast: mistaken judgment from comparing two items in sequence, where the first influences perception of the second
# Personality and Individual Differences (PID)
openness: active imagination, curiosity, and adventurousness toward new ideas and experiences
conscientiousness: thoughtfulness, impulse control, and goal-directed self-discipline
extraversion: degree of sociability, assertiveness, talkativeness, and emotional expressiveness
agreeableness: kindness, altruism, and other prosocial tendencies
neuroticism: mood swings, anxiety, and emotional instability
disorganization: tendency to act without prior planning or to tolerate disorder
freewheeling: tendency to disregard rules or conventions; disinhibition
individual indifference: sustained disinterest toward a task, especially regarding security
negligence: failure to take proper care of a task, leading to security breaches
trust: tendency to believe in or rely on others
self-control: ability to regulate decision-making despite strong emotions or desires
vulnerability: state of needing special care, support, or protection
impatience: frustration with waiting or with the time required to complete a task
impulsivity: tendency to act without forethought
submissiveness: readiness to conform to the authority or will of others
curiosity: desire to know or learn something
laziness: unwillingness to expend the effort required to complete a task
vigilance: watchfulness for potential dangers or anomalies
# Cognitive
cognitive miser: reliance on mental shortcuts when making decisions
expertise: domain knowledge about a particular subject
overconfidence: excessive confidence in one's own abilities, especially in detecting threats
absentmindedness: diversion of attention away from the current task
# Emotion
greed: intense desire for wealth, power, or material gain
fear: belief that something painful, dangerous, or threatening may happen
sympathy: understanding of another's emotional state without necessarily sharing the same feeling
empathy: personally relating to another's emotional state based on similar past experience
loneliness: perceived gap between desired and actual social companionship, connection, or intimacy
hopelessness: feeling of despair, lack of hope, or belief that things cannot be improved
# Workplace
workload: amount of work one is responsible for completing
stress: physical, emotional, or psychological strain on a person
busyness: state of having too many things to do
hurry: rushing to complete a task, which reduces adherence to secure practices
affective commitment: emotional attachment to an organization
habituation: desensitization to a repeated task or warning over time
cynicism: negative or distrustful workplace attitude, often linked to grievance or dissatisfaction
self-efficacy: belief in one's ability to produce an intended result
subjective norm: belief that important others approve, support, or expect a particular behavior
